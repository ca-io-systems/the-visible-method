## Thursday, Aug 6 · Insight one

**You are not invisible**

### Email — Thu 8am · All

**Subject:** you're not invisible

For two years I thought nobody was seeing me.

Eleven thousand followers. Eighty percent were men.

I couldn't work out why nothing happened. Nobody bought anything. My people weren't there.

Then a woman messaged me.

She said: you guys don't even realise I have these degrees. All you know is I'm a pretty girl.

She meant herself. I read it about me.

That was the day it landed.

I wasn't invisible. Eleven thousand people looked at me every day.

They were looking at the wrong thing. And I'd shown them where to look.

Sit with that difference for a second.

If nobody sees you, the fix is more. More posts. More hooks. More hours.

Everyone sells you that fix. It's exhausting. It doesn't work.

If you're seen for the wrong thing, more won't help.

Different will. And different is a decision, not a grind.

Almost every woman I've worked with was in the second one.

She was treating it like the first. That's why she was so tired.

Do this before tomorrow. Five minutes. We use it on {{custom_values.webby__event_day}}.

Open your profile. Look at your last nine posts.

Pretend you've never met yourself.

1. What would a stranger guess you do for a living?

2. What would she guess you care about?

3. What have you lived through that isn't on that grid?

Number three has the longest answer. It always does.

That answer is why we're doing this.

Tomorrow: why saying something different feels dangerous. And why that feeling is right.

Jamie

My old process for finding content went like this.

Open the app. Scroll. See what worked for other people.

Feel a bit bad. Close the app. Post nothing.

I called it research. It was doom scrolling with a job title.

In a voice that isn't yours. About a topic you don't care about.

## Monday, Aug 10 · Insight five

**You're allowed to be private and still be known**

### Email — Mon 8am · All

**Subject:** the worst advice in this entire industry

Be more raw. Be more real. Show the mess.

That was the only advice available when I started.

I think it has stopped more women from posting than any algorithm change.

I tried it. It felt wrong every time.

Not scary-wrong. That would have been useful.

Wrong like wearing someone else's coat.

And the advice was everywhere. So I assumed the problem was me.

That I wasn't ready. That I hadn't done enough work to be brave enough.

What was actually true:

The format was wrong for me. The honesty wasn't.

Those are two different things. The industry welded them together.

Somewhere we decided that truth has to be ugly.

That if it isn't messy it isn't real. That being polished is the same as hiding.

It isn't. Polished just means you decided how to say it first.

That's not dishonesty. That's care.

You're allowed to be private and be known.

You're allowed to be polished and be honest.

You're allowed to keep your page beautiful and still say something that costs you.

Those were never opposites. Being told they were has cost women years.

A client told me at the start that she's private.

She wouldn't go deep. She wanted her page to stay beautiful.

We built everything inside those rules.

She went from 16,000 followers to 78,000.

Her walls weren't the problem. Nobody had asked her where they were.

So I ask every client what they won't do before I ask what they want to say.

Want to answer that for yourself? Hit reply.

Tell me the one thing you won't do online. I read them all.

Tomorrow's the last one before we go live. It's about discipline.

Jamie

### Email — Tue 8am · All

**Subject:** nobody is disciplined

Last one before Thursday.

I worked with a woman who posted every day for six months. Every day.

She went from 16,000 followers to 78,000.

She got a La Mer collaboration. She launched a product she didn't know she wanted.

Everyone says the same thing. She must be so disciplined.

She is. That's not why it worked.

She had an editor. A virtual assistant. Someone on operations.

Every idea and file lived in one place.

And she always had about a month of content already planned.

She wasn't finding willpower every morning. There was nothing to find.

The decision happened weeks earlier. The machine just ran.

This is the most expensive misunderstanding in the whole thing.

Consistency isn't what you put in. It's what comes out.

When someone tells you to be more consistent, they're handing you the result.

Then calling it the method.

I did it the hard way first.

I batched fifteen videos. Edited a week later. Hated them. Binned the lot.

Then I got a system. An editor for four hundred dollars a month.

One folder. One board. That's the whole glamorous secret.
